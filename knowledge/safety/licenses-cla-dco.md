# Why a license matters

Public source code without a license is not automatically open source. Copyright restrictions still apply. An open-source license grants permissions under stated conditions. Follow the project's license for use, modification and distribution.

# Common license families

Permissive licenses such as MIT, BSD and Apache generally allow broad reuse while requiring notices and, depending on the license, additional conditions. Copyleft licenses such as GPL require covered distributed derivatives to remain under compatible terms. AGPL adds obligations for certain network use. Exact compatibility questions can be complex and require project or legal review.

Do not remove copyright or license notices. Do not copy code from another project solely because it is publicly visible. Check its license and compatibility first.

# Contributor License Agreement

A CLA is an agreement between a contributor and project or foundation covering rights granted for contributions. Some projects require an individual or corporate CLA before automated checks pass. Use the project's official CLA link and ensure your Git identity matches when required.

# Developer Certificate of Origin

DCO sign-off is a contributor certification attached to commits, commonly added with `git commit -s`. It is not the same as a CLA. Read the Developer Certificate of Origin before signing and do not sign for work you lack the right to contribute.

# When uncertain

Read LICENSE, NOTICE, CONTRIBUTING and legal documentation. Ask project maintainers about their policy. OLLM provides general education, not legal advice.
