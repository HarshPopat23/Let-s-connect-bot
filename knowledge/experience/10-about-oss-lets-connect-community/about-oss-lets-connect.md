# Who this community is, and who started it

OSS | Let's Connect is a contributor-driven open-source community based in India. It was founded by Sumit Goyal, with a simple vision: make open source accessible to everyone, especially new contributors. The reasoning behind that vision is direct: new contributors are not short on talent, they are short on direction. Most students and early developers who want to contribute to open source do not fail because they are not skilled enough. They fail because nobody around them can tell them where to start, how a real project actually works, or how to talk to a maintainer without feeling intimidated.

The community works across borders, meaning it is not limited to any one college, city, or country. Anyone curious about open source can join, ask questions, and learn alongside people who are already contributing to large, real, production-grade codebases.

# Why it exists

The community exists to make open source not just accessible, but meaningful and healthy. That second word, healthy, matters a lot. Anyone can open a pull request. Not every pull request is a genuine contribution. Some are auto-generated by AI with no understanding behind them, made only to inflate a contribution count. This community pushes back against that. It wants new contributors making real, understood, first contributions, not flaky PRs that maintainers have to reject and explain, again and again.

Three ideas sit at the center of everything the community does:

- Collaboration over competition. Contributors are encouraged to help each other, not treat open source as a race against other contributors.
- Bridging the gap between codebases and new contributors. Reading a large, unfamiliar codebase for the first time is genuinely hard. The community exists to make that first read less overwhelming.
- Preaching good practices, so that no group of contributors keeps repeating the same mistakes that get open-source newcomers a bad reputation in maintainer circles.

# What the community actually does

The core activity is a weekly community meeting (Saturdays, 9:30 PM IST), where common doubts are addressed live. Beyond that, the community runs sessions that walk beginners through the practical, step-by-step contribution journey: understanding what a project actually does, finding and opening a first issue, and submitting a first pull request. It has also hosted sessions with maintainers and engineers from outside the community, including guests from organizations like Red Hat, so members can hear directly from people who build and maintain real open-source projects at scale, not just from other students.

The community talks about GSoC, LFX Mentorship, Kubernetes, JSON Schema, and similar ecosystems often, simply because many of its members are actively contributing to those projects. But talking about programs is not the community's purpose. Its purpose is open source itself; see [[why-open-source-is-worth-far-more-than-any-single-program]] for why that distinction matters.

# The four stages this community thinks in

Most members' journey through open source is described using four stages:

1. Discover — figuring out where to even start.
2. Contribute — learning how real projects actually work, and making a first genuine contribution.
3. Collaborate — learning how to work and communicate with maintainers and communities professionally.
4. Grow — turning a first contribution into a long-term, lasting impact, rather than a one-time event.

# The community by the numbers

As of its first year, the community has grown to 800+ members, has supported 500+ students through their first open-source milestones, has reach across 20+ open-source projects and ecosystems, and has run 10+ knowledge-sharing sessions.

# Ecosystem exposure

Community members have contributed to and been recognized across a wide range of organisations and projects, including CNCF, Kubernetes, Kubeflow, Headlamp, Jaeger, Kiwix, JSON Schema, Meshery, PipeCD, DeepChem, Fossology, Kmesh, OpenWISP, SugarLabs, NumFOCUS, Open Science Labs, OpenSSF, Flare, and C2SI.

# What contributors say about it

New contributors consistently describe the same few things when asked what helped them: finding the community almost by chance (through LinkedIn, X, or word of mouth) and not expecting much, then being surprised by how genuinely helpful the weekly meetings and the people around them turned out to be; the lack of any unnecessary hierarchy, where anyone can ask a question or offer help regardless of experience level; and steady, real progress that came from consistency rather than speed — contributors describing their first pull request landing after weeks of small, guided steps, and others describing several merged pull requests across projects like Meshery and pandas after continued participation.

# Vision, mission, and values

Vision: make India one of the world's most trusted, collaborative, and impact-driven open-source contributor ecosystems.

Mission: create accessible pathways for developers to learn open source, make meaningful contributions, collaborate with global communities, and grow into mentors and maintainers.

Values the community repeats often: stay open (learn openly), be useful (create real value, not noise), be respectful (respect people's time and effort), and be consistent (keep showing up, since consistency compounds faster than intensity).
