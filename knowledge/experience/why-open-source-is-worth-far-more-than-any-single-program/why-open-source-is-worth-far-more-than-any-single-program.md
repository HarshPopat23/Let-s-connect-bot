# GSoC and LFX are not the point of open source

It is easy, especially early on, to start thinking of GSoC, LFX Mentorship, Outreachy or any similar program as the actual goal, and of contributing itself as just the entry ticket to that goal. That framing has it backwards. These programs are genuinely useful stepping stones, and this knowledge base covers them in detail elsewhere for exactly that reason, but they are one narrow slice of what contributing to open source actually gives you. Whether or not you ever get accepted into any paid program, contributing itself is already the valuable part.

# What you actually get, program or no program

You get to **meet new people** from completely different countries, backgrounds and companies, who you would likely never have crossed paths with otherwise, simply because you both cared about the same piece of software.

You get to **meet the people who actually created and lead the projects you use every day**, founders and maintainers of tools with real global reach, as real collaborators on a shared piece of work, not as distant public figures.

You get **real ownership** over a piece of software other people depend on. Over time, a genuine, consistent contributor is trusted with more: reviewing other people's changes, deciding on a feature's direction, sometimes eventually maintaining an entire area of the project. That is a kind of ownership very few other activities hand you this directly, this early, regardless of your age or where you went to school.

Your code ends up **read, used, and built on by developers far more experienced than you**, sometimes inside products used by millions of people, and sometimes by other great developers, precisely the kind of exposure and feedback that accelerates how fast you actually grow as an engineer.

You get **genuinely thanked by real people** whose problem your specific change actually fixed. That is a different, more durable kind of satisfaction than a certificate or a stipend, because it is proof that the thing you built mattered to someone.

# The programs are a bonus on top of all this, not the destination

None of the above depends on ever being selected for GSoC, LFX, or anything similar. If you do get selected, that is a genuine, welcome bonus on top of everything already described, not the reason contributing was worthwhile in the first place. If you do not get selected, or choose never to apply at all, everything in this section still fully applies to you. Keep this in view whenever a program's selection process starts to feel like the entire point; it never was.
